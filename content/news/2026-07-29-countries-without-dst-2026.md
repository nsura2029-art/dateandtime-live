# Countries That Don't Observe Daylight Saving Time in 2026

The twice-yearly clock change feels universal — but it's anything but. Of the **195 sovereign nations** in the world, only about **70 countries** observe any form of Daylight Saving Time in 2026. The other **125+ countries** keep their clocks the same all year.

## Where DST Is the Norm

DST remains most common in two regions:

- **North America** — United States, Canada (most provinces), Mexico (most states), plus a few Caribbean and Central American countries
- **Europe** — All 27 EU member states (the EU voted to end DST in 2019 but member states haven't implemented the change yet), the UK, Norway, and a handful of Balkan countries

In these regions, **springing forward** and **falling back** are twice-yearly rituals that touch roughly 800 million people.

## Where DST Has Been Abolished

The list of countries that have **abolished DST** in recent years includes:

- **Japan** (1951)
- **India** (never adopted)
- **China** (1991)
- **Russia** (2011)
- **Turkey** (2016)
- **Most of Africa** (Egypt is the only African country that recently observed DST)
- **Most of South America** — Brazil abolished DST in 2019; Argentina, Chile, Paraguay, and Uruguay have all eliminated or suspended it
- **Most of Asia** — including South Korea, China, Singapore, Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand

In 2019, the **European Union voted to abolish DST by 2021**, but the change was postponed indefinitely due to the COVID-19 pandemic and disagreements between member states.

## The Exceptions: Countries With DST but No One Knows Why

A few countries observe DST in unusual ways:

- **Morocco** observes DST during Ramadan, then reverts to standard time for the rest of the year
- **Iran** observes DST but with a 30-minute offset during standard time (IRST = UTC+3:30) and a separate offset during daylight (IRDT = UTC+4:30)
- **Chile** suspended DST in 2019 but reinstated it in 2024 for the Magallanes region only
- **Egypt** abolished DST in 2023, then briefly reinstated it for 2024, then abolished it again

## The U.S. State-Level Push

The most interesting DST debate is happening in the United States, where the **Sunshine Protection Act** would make DST permanent nationwide. The bill has passed the Senate multiple times but stalled in the House. As of 2026, **17 states** have passed laws to make DST permanent — but they can't take effect until federal law changes.

In the meantime, Arizona (except the Navajo Nation) and Hawaii continue to opt out of DST year-round, joining most of the world's countries on the no-DST side of history.

## Why It Matters

If you're coordinating meetings across time zones, knowing who observes DST — and when they switch — is the difference between a 9 AM call and a 7 AM one. Tools like a **meeting planner** that accounts for DST can save you from a 4 AM surprise.

For most of the world, the answer is simple: **set your clock once and forget it**.
