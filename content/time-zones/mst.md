# What is MST (Mountain Standard Time)?

**MST — Mountain Standard Time** is the standard time for the Mountain time zone in the United States. It is **7 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC−7)**.

If it's noon in London (UTC/GMT), it's 5:00 am in Denver. If it's noon in New York (EST, UTC−5), it's 10:00 am in Denver.

MST is the "winter" version of Mountain time. During daylight saving time (mid-March to early November), most Mountain-zone places switch to **MDT — Mountain Daylight Time (UTC−6)**. **One major exception: Arizona** (except the Navajo Nation) stays on MST year-round and does not observe DST.

## Where is MST used?

MST covers 8 US states fully or mostly (AZ, CO, MT, NM, UT, WY, ID-south, OR-east-NE-west, ND-southwest, SD-west). In Canada, MST is used in Alberta (in winter) and parts of BC. Mexico's states of Chihuahua, Sinaloa, Sonora, and Baja California Sur use MST in winter.

**Note on Arizona:** Most of Arizona (including Phoenix) does not observe daylight saving time. It stays on MST (UTC−7) year-round. This means in summer, Arizona is on the same time as California (PDT, UTC−7). In winter, Arizona is 1 hour behind the Pacific time zone.

## MST vs MDT — what's the difference?

| Season | Abbreviation | UTC offset | Dates (2026) | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Winter (standard) | MST | UTC−7 | Jan 1 – Mar 7, Nov 1 – Dec 31 | All Mountain zone states |
| Summer (daylight) | MDT | UTC−6 | Mar 8 – Oct 31 | All Mountain zone states EXCEPT Arizona |
| Summer (year-round MST) | MST | UTC−7 | Year-round | Arizona (most) |

The DST transition happens at 2:00 am local time. Spring forward jumps from 2:00 am to 3:00 am. Fall back goes from 2:00 am to 1:00 am.

## MST compared to other US time zones

- **1 hour ahead of PST** (Pacific Standard Time, UTC−8)
- **1 hour behind CST** (Central Standard Time, UTC−6)
- **2 hours behind EST** (Eastern Standard Time, UTC−5)
- **2 hours ahead of AKST** (Alaska Standard Time, UTC−9)
- **3 hours ahead of HST** (Hawaii Standard Time, UTC−10)

## History of Mountain Standard Time

The Mountain time zone was created on November 18, 1883, with the adoption of Standard Railway Time. The zone was originally drawn based on railroad operations, and has shifted slightly over the years as states have changed their preferences.

Arizona's exemption from DST began in 1968, after the Uniform Time Act of 1966 standardized DST nationally. Arizona opted out, citing the state's hot summer evenings — adding an extra hour of sunlight meant hotter evenings and more air conditioning.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What does MST stand for?**
A: Mountain Standard Time. It refers to the standard (non-DST) time for the Mountain time zone in North America.

**Q: Is MST the same as Phoenix time?**
A: Yes, but only because Phoenix stays on MST year-round. Other MST cities (Denver, Salt Lake City) switch to MDT in summer.

**Q: Is Arizona on MST or MDT?**
A: Most of Arizona stays on MST year-round. Only the Navajo Nation (in northeastern Arizona) observes MDT.

**Q: What's the difference between MST and MDT?**
A: 1 hour. MST is UTC−7 (winter), MDT is UTC−6 (summer).

**Q: When does MST change to MDT in 2026?**
A: At 2:00 am local time on Sunday, March 8, 2026, in Mountain-zone states that observe DST. They fall back to MST on Sunday, November 1, 2026.
