# What is EST (Eastern Standard Time)?

**EST — Eastern Standard Time** is the standard time for the Eastern time zone in the United States during the non-daylight-saving months. It is **5 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC−5)**.

If it's noon in London (UTC/GMT), it's 7:00 am in New York City. If it's noon in Tokyo, it's 10:00 pm the night before in New York.

EST is the "winter" version of Eastern time. When daylight saving time (DST) is in effect, the same places switch to **EDT — Eastern Daylight Time**, which is UTC−4. The clocks "spring forward" to EDT in March and "fall back" to EST in November.

## Where is EST used?

EST covers a huge swath of eastern North America. In the United States, it includes 17 states fully or mostly (CT, DE, FL, GA, ME, MD, MA, MI, NH, NJ, NY, NC, OH, PA, RI, SC, VT, VA, WV, DC) and parts of Indiana, Kentucky, and Tennessee.

In Canada, EST is used in Ontario, Quebec, and parts of Nunavut. Outside North America, EST is the standard time in the Bahamas, Cuba, Haiti, Jamaica, the Cayman Islands, and the Turks and Caicos.

## EST vs EDT — what's the difference?

Same Eastern time zone, different clock reading. EST is the **standard** time, used in winter. EDT is the **daylight saving** time, used in spring and summer. The difference is exactly 1 hour.

| Season | Abbreviation | UTC offset | Dates (2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winter (standard) | EST | UTC−5 | Jan 1 – Mar 7, Nov 1 – Dec 31 |
| Summer (daylight) | EDT | UTC−4 | Mar 8 – Oct 31 |

## EST compared to other US time zones

- **1 hour ahead of CST** (Central Standard Time, UTC−6)
- **2 hours ahead of MST** (Mountain Standard Time, UTC−7)
- **3 hours ahead of PST** (Pacific Standard Time, UTC−8)
- **4 hours ahead of AKST** (Alaska Standard Time, UTC−9)
- **5 hours ahead of HST** (Hawaii Standard Time, UTC−10)

## History of Eastern Standard Time

The Eastern time zone was created on November 18, 1883, when North American railroads adopted a standard time system. Before that, every city kept its own local time based on the sun, which made train scheduling chaotic.

In 1918, the Standard Time Act made time zones federal law in the US and established EDT as the daylight saving version. The Uniform Time Act of 1966 standardized the start and end dates for DST across the country, though states can opt out (Arizona and Hawaii do).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What does EST stand for?**
A: Eastern Standard Time. It refers to the standard (non-DST) time for the Eastern time zone in North America.

**Q: Is EST the same as New York time?**
A: Only in winter. New York observes EDT (UTC−4) in summer and EST (UTC−5) in winter.

**Q: What is the difference between EST and EDT?**
A: EST is UTC−5 (winter), EDT is UTC−4 (summer). They represent the same Eastern time zone, just different clock readings due to daylight saving time.

**Q: Which US states use EST?**
A: 17 states fully (CT, DE, FL, GA, ME, MD, MA, NH, NJ, NY, NC, OH, PA, RI, SC, VT, VA, WV) plus DC, plus parts of IN, KY, MI, TN.

**Q: When does EST change to EDT in 2026?**
A: At 2:00 am local time on Sunday, March 8, 2026, clocks spring forward to EDT (UTC−4). They fall back to EST on Sunday, November 1, 2026.
