# What is MDT (Mountain Daylight Time)?

**MDT — Mountain Daylight Time** is the daylight saving time for the Mountain time zone in the United States. It is **6 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC−6)**.

If it's noon in London (UTC/GMT), it's 6:00 am in Denver during MDT. The clocks are set 1 hour ahead of standard time (MST, UTC−7) to take advantage of longer daylight hours in the evening.

MDT is the "summer" version of Mountain time. From mid-March to early November, the Mountain time zone uses MDT. The rest of the year, it switches back to **MST — Mountain Standard Time (UTC−7)**.

**One major exception: Arizona** (except the Navajo Nation) does NOT observe MDT. It stays on MST year-round.

## When is MDT in effect?

MDT runs from the second Sunday in March through the first Sunday in November. In 2026, that's **March 8 to November 1**.

| Year | Spring forward (MST → MDT) | Fall back (MDT → MST) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | Sun, Mar 8 at 2:00 am | Sun, Nov 1 at 2:00 am |
| 2027 | Sun, Mar 14 at 2:00 am | Sun, Nov 7 at 2:00 am |
| 2028 | Sun, Mar 12 at 2:00 am | Sun, Nov 5 at 2:00 am |

At 2:00 am local time on the spring forward date, clocks jump directly to 3:00 am. The hour from 2:00 to 3:00 am does not exist on that day.

## Where is MDT used?

The same Mountain-zone places that use MST in winter switch to MDT in summer. That includes 7 US states fully or mostly: CO, MT, NM, UT, WY, plus parts of ID, ND, OR, SD. In Canada, Alberta (in summer) and parts of BC use MDT. Mexico's Chihuahua state uses MDT in summer.

**Arizona does NOT observe MDT.** Phoenix and most of Arizona stay on MST (UTC−7) year-round. This means during summer, Arizona is on the same time as California (PDT, UTC−7). During winter, Arizona is 1 hour behind California (PST, UTC−8).

## Why daylight saving time?

The idea behind DST is to shift daylight hours. By moving the clock forward 1 hour in spring, sunset happens 1 hour later in the evening, giving people more daylight after work or school.

Arizona opted out of DST in 1968, after the Uniform Time Act of 1966 standardized DST nationally. The state's hot summer evenings were the cited reason — extra daylight meant hotter evenings and more air conditioning.

## MDT compared to other time zones

- **1 hour ahead of PDT** (Pacific Daylight Time, UTC−7)
- **1 hour behind CDT** (Central Daylight Time, UTC−5)
- **2 hours behind EDT** (Eastern Daylight Time, UTC−4)
- **2 hours ahead of AKDT** (Alaska Daylight Time, UTC−8)
- **3 hours ahead of HST** (Hawaii Standard Time, UTC−10)

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What does MDT stand for?**
A: Mountain Daylight Time. It's the daylight saving time version of Mountain time, UTC−6, used in summer.

**Q: Is MDT the same as Denver time?**
A: Only in summer. Denver observes MDT (UTC−6) in summer and MST (UTC−7) in winter.

**Q: Does Arizona observe MDT?**
A: No. Most of Arizona stays on MST year-round. Only the Navajo Nation (in northeastern AZ) observes MDT.

**Q: When does MDT start in 2026?**
A: Sunday, March 8, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks spring forward to 3:00 am.

**Q: When does MDT end in 2026?**
A: Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks fall back to 1:00 am, returning to MST.
