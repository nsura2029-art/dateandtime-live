# What is PDT (Pacific Daylight Time)?

**PDT — Pacific Daylight Time** is the daylight saving time for the Pacific time zone in the United States. It is **7 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC−7)**.

If it's noon in London (UTC/GMT), it's 5:00 am in Los Angeles during PDT. The clocks are set 1 hour ahead of standard time (PST, UTC−8) to take advantage of longer daylight hours in the evening.

PDT is the "summer" version of Pacific time. From mid-March to early November, the Pacific time zone uses PDT. The rest of the year, it switches back to **PST — Pacific Standard Time (UTC−8)**.

## When is PDT in effect?

PDT runs from the second Sunday in March through the first Sunday in November. In 2026, that's **March 8 to November 1**.

| Year | Spring forward (PST → PDT) | Fall back (PDT → PST) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | Sun, Mar 8 at 2:00 am | Sun, Nov 1 at 2:00 am |
| 2027 | Sun, Mar 14 at 2:00 am | Sun, Nov 7 at 2:00 am |
| 2028 | Sun, Mar 12 at 2:00 am | Sun, Nov 5 at 2:00 am |

At 2:00 am local time on the spring forward date, clocks jump directly to 3:00 am. The hour from 2:00 to 3:00 am does not exist on that day.

## Where is PDT used?

The same places that use PST in winter switch to PDT in summer. That includes 5 US states fully or mostly: CA, WA, OR, NV, ID (northern part). In Canada, British Columbia observes PDT in summer.

**Yukon Territory, Canada** does NOT observe PDT. It stays on year-round MST/PST (technically UTC−7).

## Why daylight saving time?

The idea behind DST is to shift daylight hours. By moving the clock forward 1 hour in spring, sunset happens 1 hour later in the evening (e.g., 8 pm instead of 7 pm), giving people more daylight after work or school.

DST was first proposed by Benjamin Franklin in 1784. Germany was the first country to adopt it nationally in 1916, to save coal during World War I. The US followed in 1918.

In 2018, California voters passed Proposition 7, which authorizes the state legislature to make DST permanent — but only if federal law changes to allow it. The Sunshine Protection Act has been proposed multiple times in Congress but has not passed.

## PDT compared to other time zones

- **1 hour behind MDT** (Mountain Daylight Time, UTC−6)
- **2 hours behind CDT** (Central Daylight Time, UTC−5)
- **3 hours behind EDT** (Eastern Daylight Time, UTC−4)
- **1 hour ahead of AKDT** (Alaska Daylight Time, UTC−8)
- **2 hours ahead of HST** (Hawaii Standard Time, UTC−10)

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What does PDT stand for?**
A: Pacific Daylight Time. It's the daylight saving time version of Pacific time, UTC−7, used in summer.

**Q: Is PDT the same as Los Angeles time?**
A: Only in summer. Los Angeles observes PDT (UTC−7) in summer and PST (UTC−8) in winter.

**Q: When does PDT start in 2026?**
A: Sunday, March 8, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks spring forward to 3:00 am.

**Q: When does PDT end in 2026?**
A: Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks fall back to 1:00 am, returning to PST.

**Q: Will the US abolish DST?**
A: As of 2026, no. The Sunshine Protection Act has been proposed but not passed. Several states have authorized permanent DST if federal law changes.
