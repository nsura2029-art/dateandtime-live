# What is EDT (Eastern Daylight Time)?

**EDT — Eastern Daylight Time** is the daylight saving time for the Eastern time zone in the United States. It is **4 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC−4)**.

If it's noon in London (UTC/GMT), it's 8:00 am in New York City during EDT. The clocks are set 1 hour ahead of standard time to take advantage of longer daylight hours in the evening.

EDT is the "summer" version of Eastern time. From mid-March to early November, the Eastern time zone uses EDT. The rest of the year (early November to mid-March), it switches back to **EST — Eastern Standard Time (UTC−5)**.

## When is EDT in effect?

EDT runs from the second Sunday in March through the first Sunday in November. In 2026, that's **March 8 to November 1**.

| Year | Spring forward (EST → EDT) | Fall back (EDT → EST) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | Sun, Mar 8 at 2:00 am | Sun, Nov 1 at 2:00 am |
| 2027 | Sun, Mar 14 at 2:00 am | Sun, Nov 7 at 2:00 am |
| 2028 | Sun, Mar 12 at 2:00 am | Sun, Nov 5 at 2:00 am |

At 2:00 am local time on the spring forward date, clocks jump directly to 3:00 am. The hour from 2:00 to 3:00 am does not exist on that day.

## Where is EDT used?

The same places that use EST in winter switch to EDT in summer. That includes 17 US states fully or mostly, plus DC, plus parts of Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, and Tennessee. In Canada, Ontario and Quebec observe EDT. So do the Bahamas, Cuba, Haiti, Jamaica, the Cayman Islands, and the Turks and Caicos.

## Why daylight saving time?

The idea behind DST is to shift daylight hours. By moving the clock forward 1 hour in spring, sunset happens 1 hour later in the evening (e.g., 8 pm instead of 7 pm), giving people more daylight after work or school.

DST was first proposed by Benjamin Franklin in 1784 (in a satirical letter to a Paris journal). Germany was the first country to adopt it nationally in 1916, to save coal during World War I. The US followed in 1918.

Today, daylight saving time is observed in most of the US, Canada, Mexico (most), and Europe. It is NOT observed in Arizona (except the Navajo Nation) or Hawaii.

## EDT compared to other time zones

- **1 hour ahead of CDT** (Central Daylight Time, UTC−5)
- **2 hours ahead of MDT** (Mountain Daylight Time, UTC−6)
- **3 hours ahead of PDT** (Pacific Daylight Time, UTC−7)
- **4 hours ahead of AKDT** (Alaska Daylight Time, UTC−8)
- **5 hours ahead of HDT** (Hawaii-Aleutian Daylight Time, UTC−9, only observed in Adak, Alaska)

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What does EDT stand for?**
A: Eastern Daylight Time. It's the daylight saving time version of Eastern time, UTC−4, used in summer.

**Q: Is EDT the same as New York time?**
A: Only in summer. New York observes EDT (UTC−4) in summer and EST (UTC−5) in winter.

**Q: When does EDT start in 2026?**
A: Sunday, March 8, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks spring forward to 3:00 am.

**Q: When does EDT end in 2026?**
A: Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks fall back to 1:00 am, returning to EST.

**Q: Is daylight saving time being abolished?**
A: As of 2026, no. The "Sunshine Protection Act" has been proposed multiple times in the US Congress but has not passed. Some states (e.g., California, Florida) have voted to make DST permanent if federal law changes.
