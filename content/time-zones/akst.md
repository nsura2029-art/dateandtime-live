# What is AKST (Alaska Standard Time)?

**AKST — Alaska Standard Time** is the standard time for most of Alaska during the non-daylight-saving months. It is **9 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC−9)**.

If it's noon in London (UTC/GMT), it's 3:00 am in Anchorage. If it's noon in New York (EST, UTC−5), it's 8:00 am in Anchorage.

AKST is the "winter" version of Alaska time. During daylight saving time (mid-March to early November), most of Alaska switches to **AKDT — Alaska Daylight Time (UTC−8)**.

## Where is AKST used?

AKST covers most of Alaska, but not all of it. There are technically two IANA timezones in Alaska:

- **America/Anchorage** — most of Alaska, including Anchorage, Fairbanks, Juneau. Observes DST.
- **America/Adak** — the western Aleutian Islands. Observes DST but at HAST/HADT (UTC−10/UTC−9).
- **Pacific/Honolulu** — technically Alaska has no zone that stays on year-round standard time, except for very small areas.

The 51st state, Hawaii, is in HST (UTC−10) year-round, not AKST.

## Major cities in the AKST zone

## AKST vs AKDT — what's the difference?

| Season | Abbreviation | UTC offset | Dates (2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winter (standard) | AKST | UTC−9 | Jan 1 – Mar 7, Nov 1 – Dec 31 |
| Summer (daylight) | AKDT | UTC−8 | Mar 8 – Oct 31 |

Alaska is famous for its extreme daylight. In summer, Anchorage can have 22 hours of daylight. In winter, it can have less than 6 hours. DST shifts an extra hour of evening daylight in summer.

## AKST compared to other US time zones

- **1 hour ahead of HDT** (Hawaii-Aleutian Daylight Time, UTC−9, observed only in Adak)
- **1 hour behind PST** (Pacific Standard Time, UTC−8)
- **4 hours behind EST** (Eastern Standard Time, UTC−5)
- **5 hours behind EDT** (Eastern Daylight Time, UTC−4)
- **Same as HDT** (Hawaii-Aleutian Daylight Time, observed only in Adak, AK)

## History of Alaska Standard Time

Alaska has had a complex time zone history. Before the Standard Time Act of 1918, Alaska used four different time zones. The 1918 act reduced that to two: Alaska time (UTC−10) and Bering time (UTC−11).

In 1966, the Uniform Time Act standardized DST, but Alaska didn't observe DST until 1980, when the state legislature approved it. In 1983, Alaska's time zone boundaries were redrawn, and the Anchorage zone (UTC−9 winter, UTC−8 summer) was officially established.

In 2015, a poll showed Alaskans favored staying on AKDT (summer time) year-round, but the state has not formally made the change.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What does AKST stand for?**
A: Alaska Standard Time. It refers to the standard (non-DST) time for most of Alaska.

**Q: Is Alaska on the same time as California?**
A: Only in summer, when California is on PDT (UTC−7) and Alaska is on AKDT (UTC−8). In winter, California is on PST (UTC−8) and Alaska is on AKST (UTC−9) — 1 hour behind.

**Q: Does Alaska observe DST?**
A: Most of Alaska observes DST, switching between AKST and AKDT. The far western Aleutian Islands (Adak) are in a different zone (HAST/HADT, UTC−10/UTC−9).

**Q: When does AKST change to AKDT in 2026?**
A: At 2:00 am local time on Sunday, March 8, 2026, clocks spring forward to AKDT (UTC−8). They fall back to AKST on Sunday, November 1, 2026.

**Q: Is Alaska time the same as Hawaii time?**
A: No. Alaska is 1 hour ahead of Hawaii (HST is UTC−10, AKST is UTC−9).
