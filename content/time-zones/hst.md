# What is HST (Hawaii Standard Time)?

**HST — Hawaii Standard Time** is the standard time for the state of Hawaii. It is **10 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC−10)**.

If it's noon in London (UTC/GMT), it's 2:00 am in Honolulu. If it's noon in New York (EST, UTC−5), it's 7:00 am in Honolulu.

**Hawaii does NOT observe daylight saving time.** HST is in effect year-round. There is no "HDT" abbreviation in common use because Hawaii doesn't switch.

## Where is HST used?

HST is the standard time for the entire state of Hawaii, including:

- **Oahu** (Honolulu, Pearl Harbor)
- **Maui** (Lahaina, Wailuku)
- **Big Island Hawaii** (Hilo, Kona)
- **Kauai** (Lihue)
- **Molokai** and **Lanai**

HST is also used in some parts of the Pacific that are politically distinct from the US, including the Cook Islands (in the southern hemisphere, but observe UTC−10 year-round, similar to Hawaii).

## Why doesn't Hawaii observe DST?

Hawaii is the only US state that doesn't observe daylight saving time, along with most of Arizona. The reasons are:

1. **Tropical latitude**: Hawaii is at 19-22°N, much closer to the equator than the mainland US. Day length varies by only about 2.5 hours throughout the year (from ~13.5 hours in summer to ~11 hours in winter). DST wouldn't save much daylight.

2. **Tourism and trade**: Hawaii's economy depends on tourism and trade with the mainland US. Switching time zones would create scheduling confusion. Staying on HST year-round is simpler.

3. **Sunrise times**: Even without DST, Hawaii's earliest sunrise is around 5:50 am (in June) and latest is around 7:10 am (in January) — a 1 hour 20 minute variation. The "extra" hour of DST would push sunrise to 6:50 am, which is later than many mainland sunrises even without DST.

Hawaii officially opted out of DST in 1967, after the Uniform Time Act of 1966 standardized DST nationally. The state has considered rejoining DST, but multiple bills have not passed.

## HST compared to other US time zones

- **1 hour ahead of AKST** (Alaska Standard Time, UTC−9)
- **2 hours ahead of PST** (Pacific Standard Time, UTC−8)
- **3 hours ahead of MST** (Mountain Standard Time, UTC−7)
- **4 hours ahead of CST** (Central Standard Time, UTC−6)
- **5 hours ahead of EST** (Eastern Standard Time, UTC−5)

So when it's noon in New York (EST), it's 7:00 am in Honolulu (HST). When it's noon in Honolulu, it's 5:00 pm in New York.

## HST and the date line

Hawaii is on the western side of the International Date Line, so it's a day "behind" most of the Americas. When it's Tuesday in New York, it's still Monday in Honolulu. This makes scheduling calls and business meetings between Hawaii and the US mainland a little tricky — Hawaii is typically the "first to start the day" and the "last to finish it" in the US.

## History of Hawaii Standard Time

Before Hawaii became a US state in 1959, the islands had no formal time zone. They used local solar time. After statehood, Hawaii was placed in the HST zone (UTC−10) and the time was standardized.

The Uniform Time Act of 1966 required all states to observe DST, but it allowed states to opt out. Hawaii opted out in 1967 and has remained on year-round HST since.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What does HST stand for?**
A: Hawaii Standard Time. It's the standard time for Hawaii, used year-round.

**Q: Does Hawaii observe daylight saving time?**
A: No. Hawaii is one of two US states that does not observe DST (the other is most of Arizona).

**Q: What is the difference between HST and EST?**
A: 5 hours. EST is 5 hours ahead of HST. When it's noon EST (New York), it's 7:00 am HST (Honolulu).

**Q: What about HDT — Hawaii Daylight Time?**
A: There is no HDT because Hawaii doesn't observe DST. The abbreviation is sometimes informally used, but Hawaii stays on HST year-round.

**Q: When is the earliest sunset in Hawaii?**
A: Around late November, sunset is approximately 5:50 pm in Honolulu. In summer, sunset is around 7:15 pm.
