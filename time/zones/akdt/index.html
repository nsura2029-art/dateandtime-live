About Alaska Daylight Time

AKDT — Alaska Daylight Time is the daylight saving time for most of Alaska. It is 8 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC−8).

If it's noon in London (UTC/GMT), it's 4:00 am in Anchorage during AKDT. The clocks are set 1 hour ahead of standard time (AKST, UTC−9) to take advantage of Alaska's extremely long summer daylight hours.

AKDT is the "summer" version of Alaska time. From mid-March to early November, most of Alaska uses AKDT. The rest of the year, it switches back to AKST — Alaska Standard Time (UTC−9).

When is AKDT in effect?

AKDT runs from the second Sunday in March through the first Sunday in November. In 2026, that's March 8 to November 1.

At 2:00 am local time on the spring forward date, clocks jump directly to 3:00 am. The hour from 2:00 to 3:00 am does not exist on that day.

Why daylight saving matters in Alaska

Alaska has some of the most extreme daylight variations in the world:

  • Summer solstice (June 21): Anchorage can have up to 22 hours of daylight. Fairbanks can have 24 hours (the sun barely sets).
  • Winter solstice (December 21): Anchorage has about 5.5 hours of daylight. Fairbanks has about 3.5 hours.

DST shifts an extra hour of evening daylight in summer, which Alaskans value for outdoor activities. In 2015, a poll found 73% of Alaskans favored staying on AKDT year-round, but the state has not formally made the change.

Where is AKDT used?

Most of Alaska, including:

  • Anchorage (largest city, 290K)
  • Fairbanks (interior, 32K)
  • Juneau (state capital, 32K)
  • Wasilla (Matanuska-Susitna Borough, 9K)
  • Sitka (southeast, 8K)

The far western Aleutian Islands (Adak, Atka) are in a different zone called HAST/HADT, which is UTC−10/UTC−9.

AKDT compared to other time zones

  • 1 hour behind PDT (Pacific Daylight Time, UTC−7)
  • 2 hours behind MDT (Mountain Daylight Time, UTC−6)
  • 3 hours behind CDT (Central Daylight Time, UTC−5)
  • 4 hours behind EDT (Eastern Daylight Time, UTC−4)
  • 1 hour ahead of HDT (Hawaii-Aleutian Daylight Time, UTC−9, observed only in Adak, AK)

Frequently asked questions

Q: What does AKDT stand for? A: Alaska Daylight Time. It's the daylight saving time version of Alaska time, UTC−8, used in summer.

Q: Is AKDT the same as Anchorage time? A: Only in summer. Anchorage observes AKDT (UTC−8) in summer and AKST (UTC−9) in winter.

Q: When does AKDT start in 2026? A: Sunday, March 8, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks spring forward to 3:00 am.

Q: When does AKDT end in 2026? A: Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks fall back to 1:00 am, returning to AKST.

Q: Will Alaska abolish DST? A: As of 2026, no. A 2015 ballot initiative showed 73% support but didn't go to a vote. The Alaska State Legislature has not passed a permanent-DST law.
