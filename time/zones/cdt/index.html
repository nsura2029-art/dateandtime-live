About Central Daylight Time

CDT — Central Daylight Time is the daylight saving time for the Central time zone in the United States. It is 5 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC−5).

If it's noon in London (UTC/GMT), it's 7:00 am in Chicago during CDT. The clocks are set 1 hour ahead of standard time (CST, UTC−6) to take advantage of longer daylight hours in the evening.

CDT is the "summer" version of Central time. From mid-March to early November, the Central time zone uses CDT. The rest of the year, it switches back to CST — Central Standard Time (UTC−6).

When is CDT in effect?

CDT runs from the second Sunday in March through the first Sunday in November. In 2026, that's March 8 to November 1.

At 2:00 am local time on the spring forward date, clocks jump directly to 3:00 am. The hour from 2:00 to 3:00 am does not exist on that day.

Where is CDT used?

The same places that use CST in winter switch to CDT in summer. That includes 9 US states fully (AL, AR, IL, IA, LA, MN, MS, MO, WI) plus parts of Kansas, Kentucky, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, and Nebraska. In Canada, Manitoba observes CDT in summer (Saskatchewan does not). Mexico City and most of Mexico use CDT in summer.

Why daylight saving time?

The idea behind DST is to shift daylight hours. By moving the clock forward 1 hour in spring, sunset happens 1 hour later in the evening (e.g., 8 pm instead of 7 pm), giving people more daylight after work or school.

DST was first proposed by Benjamin Franklin in 1784 (in a satirical letter to a Paris journal). Germany was the first country to adopt it nationally in 1916, to save coal during World War I. The US followed in 1918.

CDT compared to other time zones

  • 1 hour ahead of MDT (Mountain Daylight Time, UTC−6)
  • 1 hour behind EDT (Eastern Daylight Time, UTC−4)
  • 2 hours ahead of PDT (Pacific Daylight Time, UTC−7)
  • 3 hours ahead of AKDT (Alaska Daylight Time, UTC−8)
  • 4 hours ahead of HST (Hawaii Standard Time, UTC−10)

Frequently asked questions

Q: What does CDT stand for? A: Central Daylight Time. It's the daylight saving time version of Central time, UTC−5, used in summer.

Q: Is CDT the same as Chicago time? A: Only in summer. Chicago observes CDT (UTC−5) in summer and CST (UTC−6) in winter.

Q: When does CDT start in 2026? A: Sunday, March 8, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks spring forward to 3:00 am.

Q: When does CDT end in 2026? A: Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 am. Clocks fall back to 1:00 am, returning to CST.

Q: Does Mexico City use CDT? A: Yes, Mexico City and most of Mexico observe CDT (UTC−5) in summer, switching to CST (UTC−6) in winter on the same dates as the US.
